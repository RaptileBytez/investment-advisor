**Volatility** measures how much a stock's daily returns deviate from their
average. It's reported here as an *annualised* figure (daily standard
deviation multiplied by √252).

A volatility of **20 % p.a.** roughly means that in any given year you can
expect the stock to swing about 20 % up or down around its trend, most of
the time.

### Why it matters

- Higher volatility = larger short-term swings, including drawdowns.
- Volatility is a *symmetric* measure — it doesn't distinguish up moves
  from down moves. Pair it with **Max Drawdown** and **VaR** for a fuller
  picture.
- It's the denominator in the **Sharpe Ratio** — without volatility,
  comparing returns across stocks is meaningless.

### Rule of thumb

- Below ~15 % p.a. → typical for blue-chip equities and broad ETFs.
- 20–35 % p.a. → typical individual large-cap stock.
- Above 40 % p.a. → high-vol; growth / small-cap territory.
