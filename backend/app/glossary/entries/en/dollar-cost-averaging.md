**Dollar-Cost Averaging** is a scheduling strategy: invest the same
amount at regular intervals (monthly, biweekly), regardless of where the
price is.

### What it solves

The biggest source of underperformance is **bad timing** — buying high
and panic-selling low. DCA removes the timing decision: you simply buy
every month.

When prices are low, your fixed amount buys *more* shares; when prices
are high, *fewer*. The result is an **average purchase price** that's
below the simple average price over the period.

### When it shines

- Volatile assets you want long-term exposure to.
- Periods of high uncertainty when "wait for the dip" psychology often
  causes investors to miss the entry entirely.

### When it doesn't

In a relentless bull market, **lump-sum often beats DCA** because the
DCA amount sits in cash earning nothing while the market rises. The
DCA strategy here will show you both numbers side by side for the
backtested period.

### Limitations

- DCA can't rescue a fundamentally bad asset. If the price falls because
  the business is broken, every DCA purchase locks in more loss.
- It reduces *timing* risk but not *market* risk. Diversification still
  matters.
