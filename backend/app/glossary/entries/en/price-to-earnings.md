title: Price-to-Earnings (P/E) ratio
short: How much investors pay per €1 of annual earnings.
related: [price-to-book, dividend-yield, value-investing]
---

**P/E** = Share price ÷ Earnings per share. It tells you how many years
of current earnings you're paying for, in one number.

### Rules of thumb

| P/E   | Common interpretation                                |
| ----- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| < 10  | Often cyclicals or out-of-favour names.              |
| 10–20 | Typical mature business.                             |
| 20–40 | Growth-priced.                                       |
| > 40  | Either rapid-growth, or unprofitable on a P/E basis. |

### Trailing vs. forward

- **Trailing P/E** uses the past 12 months of earnings — concrete but
  backwards-looking.
- **Forward P/E** uses analysts' next-12-months estimates — useful but
  reflects analyst opinion.

### Caveats

- Companies with negative earnings have **no meaningful P/E**.
- Sector matters: a P/E of 15 is cheap for software and expensive for
  cyclicals. Sector-relative comparisons (coming in a future release)
  are stronger than absolute thresholds.
