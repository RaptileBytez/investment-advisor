**Diversification** is the practice of holding multiple assets so that
no single failure can destroy your portfolio.

The mathematical version of this is **Modern Portfolio Theory**: by
combining assets with imperfect correlation, you can lower portfolio
volatility *without* lowering expected return — sometimes called the
"only free lunch in finance".

### How we measure it

This advisor reports a **Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI)** for your
portfolio:

| HHI            | Concentration                                       |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| 1.0            | Single position — fully concentrated.               |
| 0.25           | Four equal positions.                               |
| 0.10           | Ten equal positions.                                |
| < 0.05         | Highly diversified.                                 |

It also reports **currency exposure** — a portfolio that's 90 % in USD
assets has implicit FX risk to a EUR-denominated investor.

### Limits of diversification

- **Correlation rises in a crisis.** Assets that look uncorrelated in
  normal markets can move together in panics (March 2020, 2008-09).
  Diversification reduces but doesn't eliminate left-tail risk.
- **Over-diversification** dilutes conviction. A retail portfolio of
  100 names usually adds noise without removing meaningful risk past
  ~20–30 positions.
