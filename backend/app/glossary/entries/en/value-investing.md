**Value investing** is the practice of finding stocks trading below
their intrinsic worth — buying €1 of business for €0.70.

### The signals we use

| Signal              | Bullish when…                       |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Trailing P/E        | low (cheap on earnings)             |
| Price/Book          | low (cheap on assets)               |
| Debt/Equity         | low (financial flexibility)         |
| Dividend yield      | moderate-to-high (3 – 6 %)          |
| Free cash-flow yield| high (cash earnings, not paper)     |

Each signal contributes a small vote; the composite drives the verdict.

### Why it works (historically)

- Cheap valuations have *mean-reverted* upward over decade-plus
  horizons — but not every quarter, and not every cycle.
- Quality-screened value (avoiding the "value traps" — see below) has
  outperformed the broad market over very long periods, with bigger
  drawdowns.

### Value traps

A stock with a low P/E and high dividend yield can be cheap *because
the business is dying*. Common warning signs:

- Persistently negative free-cash-flow growth.
- Rising debt-to-equity.
- Industry-wide pressure (tobacco, traditional retail in some markets).

This advisor flags some of these but does not screen all of them. Treat
the value verdict as a starting point for research, not as a stand-alone
decision.
